### 1. OVERVIEW & ZERO-KNOWLEDGE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

Gbrowse is a desktop browser profile management application engineered from the ground up on the foundational principle of **Zero-Knowledge, Local-Only Data Processing**. 

We believe that your browsing activity, profile metadata, container configurations, and credentials are exclusively your private property. 

* **No Developer Servers:** We do not own, operate, or maintain any telemetry, analytics, tracking, or cloud-synchronization servers.
* **No Telemetry or Tracking:** Gbrowse contains zero analytics SDKs, zero usage tracking, zero background heartbeat pings, and zero automated crash reporting transmitters.
* **No Account Creation:** Using Gbrowse does not require creating an account, registering an email address, or authenticating with an external licensing authority.
* **Local-First Architecture:** All profile metadata, browsing caches, cookies, extensions, workspaces, and encrypted credentials reside solely on your physical host machine.

This Privacy Policy explains how Gbrowse operates on your device, how your data is secured locally, the nature of user-initiated network requests, and your rights under global privacy regulations, including the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CCPA/CPRA), the California Online Privacy Protection Act (CalOPPA), and Microsoft Store Certification Policy 10.5.

### 2. DATA PROCESSED LOCALLY ON YOUR DEVICE

Gbrowse manages browser profile environments. All information required to operate these profiles is processed and stored exclusively on your local storage drive under your Windows user account directory (specifically `%APPDATA%\gbrowse` on Microsoft Windows).

The following categories of data are managed strictly within your local device environment:

1. **Profile Metadata & Organization:**
   * User-defined profile names, assigned workspaces, folder groups (`groups.json`), and custom tags.
   * Emulation configurations: User-Agent strings, IANA timezone identifiers, geolocation coordinates, and screen resolution presets.
   * Profile operational state: Local process identifiers (PIDs), start/stop timestamps, and exit diagnostic codes (`runtime.json`).

2. **Browser Storage & Session Sandboxes:**
   * Dedicated profile user data directories (`%APPDATA%\gbrowse\profiles\<profile_id>\`) containing isolated browser cookies, HTTP cache, IndexedDB, local storage, history, and browser preferences.
   * **Total Container Isolation:** Each profile operates in its own separate filesystem boundary. Data is never shared or bled across profiles or with your system's default browser.

3. **Proxy Configurations & Encrypted Credentials:**
   * User-supplied proxy network hostnames, port numbers, proxy protocols (HTTP, HTTPS, SOCKS5), and optional proxy usernames.
   * **Proxy Passwords:** Proxy passwords are encrypted using the native **Microsoft Windows Data Protection API (DPAPI)** (`CryptProtectData`) and written as ciphertext files (`.enc`) in `%APPDATA%\gbrowse\credentials\`. Decryption (`CryptUnprotectData`) occurs exclusively in-memory on your local CPU when a browser instance is launched or an explicit proxy test is requested. Plaintext passwords are never stored in readable configuration files or exported in portable backups.

### 3. NETWORK COMMUNICATIONS & THIRD-PARTY DESTINATIONS

Gbrowse does **not** transmit personal data or telemetry to the developers. However, because Gbrowse is a browser management and network utility, outbound network communication occurs under specific, user-initiated circumstances:

#### A. User-Directed Browsing Activity
When you launch a browser container (Google Chrome, Chromium, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, Brave, or Safari WebKit mode), the spawned browser process connects to the websites and web services you navigate to. 
* Any data transmitted during your browsing sessions is governed exclusively by the privacy policies of the websites you visit and your internet service or proxy provider.
* Gbrowse does not inspect, log, proxy, or intercept the content of your HTTP/HTTPS traffic.

#### B. User-Configured Proxy Routing
If you assign an HTTP, HTTPS, or SOCKS5 proxy to a profile, all network traffic generated by that browser container is routed through the proxy server you specified.
* Gbrowse creates a transient, local unpacked browser authentication extension inside the profile directory to authenticate with your proxy without exposing credentials in command-line arguments.
* Your proxy traffic is subject to the terms and privacy practices of your chosen proxy provider.

#### C. User-Initiated Proxy Health & Latency Testing
When you explicitly click the **"Test Proxy"** feature in the application interface, Gbrowse performs a diagnostic health check to verify connectivity, measure round-trip latency, and display your egress IP address:
* The application issues a single HTTP request through your configured proxy to `http://api.ipify.org?format=json`.
* This request is executed solely to retrieve your egress IP address and compute connection latency for immediate display in your UI.
* The returned IP address is kept in transient application memory and is never logged to disk or sent to the developers.

#### D. Optional 1-Click Browser Engine Downloads
Gbrowse provides an optional standalone browser installer. If you choose to download an isolated browser binary through this feature, Gbrowse retrieves verified archives directly from the official content delivery networks (CDNs) of the respective browser vendors:
* **Chromium (Chrome for Testing):** Downloaded directly from Google Cloud Storage (`storage.googleapis.com`).
* **Mozilla Firefox:** Downloaded directly from official Playwright / Microsoft Azure CDN (`playwright.azureedge.net`).
* **Brave Browser:** Downloaded directly from official Brave GitHub Releases (`github.com/brave/brave-browser`).
* No personal identifying information is transmitted during these file downloads beyond the standard TCP/IP handshake required to retrieve the public archive.

#### E. Local Chrome DevTools Protocol (CDP) Loopback
To apply per-profile emulation settings (timezone, geolocation coordinates, and screen metrics) without modifying your host operating system, Gbrowse communicates with Chromium via the Chrome DevTools Protocol (CDP).
* **Strict Loopback Binding:** CDP debugging sessions are strictly bound to `127.0.0.1` (localhost) with `--remote-debugging-address=127.0.0.1`.
* CDP sockets are strictly closed to the external network and communicate exclusively within your local machine.

### 4. DATA SECURITY & LOCAL SAFEGUARDS

In accordance with Microsoft Store Policy 10.5.4 and global security standards, Gbrowse employs robust security mechanisms:

1. **Hardware-Tied OS Encryption (Windows DPAPI):**
   Proxy credentials are encrypted using the Windows `CryptProtectData` Win32 API. Cryptographic keys are derived from your Windows user logon credentials and managed directly by the Windows Local Security Authority Subsystem Service (LSASS). Other Windows user accounts or external systems cannot decrypt these credential files.
2. **Process Ownership & Termination Guard:**
   Before terminating browser processes, Gbrowse inspects process command-line arguments via `NtQueryInformationProcess` to verify that the target PID belongs specifically to the profile's dedicated `user-data-dir`, preventing accidental termination of unrelated system processes.
3. **Atomic File Persistence:**
   All configuration files (`profile.json`, `groups.json`, `workspaces.json`) are written atomically using `AtomicFileUtil` via Windows `MoveFileExW` with `MOVEFILE_WRITE_THROUGH`, eliminating configuration corruption in the event of unexpected power loss.
4. **Strict Content Security Policy (CSP):**
   The application UI runs within a hardened Webview2 container with a strict Content Security Policy (`default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; connect-src 'self' ipc:; img-src 'self' data:;`), preventing arbitrary remote code execution and unauthorized outbound network calls.
5. **Single-Instance Mutex:**
   Gbrowse enforces a Windows Named Mutex (`Local\gbrowse_app_lock_<hash>`) and advisory lockfile (`gbrowse.lock`) to prevent concurrent process collisions and SQLite/database locking conflicts.

### 5. USER CONTROLS, DATA RETENTION & DELETION

You maintain complete sovereignty over all data created in Gbrowse:

* **Profile Deletion:** Deleting a profile from within the Gbrowse user interface permanently removes the profile metadata, the entire browser user-data directory (including all cookies, history, and cache), and the DPAPI-encrypted credential file from your filesystem.
* **Data Export:** The "Export Configuration" feature generates a portable, clean JSON file containing profile organization, emulation settings, and proxy endpoints. In accordance with zero-knowledge principles, **all session cookies, browsing cache, raw ciphertext, and plaintext passwords are permanently excluded from exports**.
* **Complete Uninstallation:** Uninstalling Gbrowse removes application binaries. To completely purge all stored profile configurations and browser containers, simply delete the local application data directory located at:
  `%APPDATA%\gbrowse` (or `C:\Users\<YourUsername>\AppData\Roaming\gbrowse`).

### 6. GLOBAL REGULATORY COMPLIANCE

#### A. European Economic Area (EEA) & UK GDPR Disclosures
* **Data Controller Status:** Under Article 4(7) of the GDPR, Gbrowse is not a Data Controller for any personal data processed within your local browser profiles. You are the sole controller of your profile data.
* **Legal Basis for Processing:** To the extent any local processing occurs (e.g. reading configuration files from disk), it is executed pursuant to Article 6(1)(b) (performance of a contract / executing requested application functionality).
* **Data Subject Rights (Articles 15–22):** You have the right to access, rectify, port, and erase your personal data. Because all data is stored locally on your machine, you can exercise these rights directly without contacting us, simply by managing, exporting, or deleting your profiles within the application or via Windows File Explorer.
* **Supervisory Authority:** You have the right to lodge a complaint with your local data protection authority if you believe your rights have been infringed.

#### B. California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA / CPRA) & CalOPPA
* **Zero Sale or Sharing of Personal Information:** We have not sold, shared, or rented personal information to third parties for monetary or other valuable consideration in the preceding 12 months. We do not sell or share personal information of consumers of any age.
* **Categories of Data Collected (Preceding 12 Months):** None collected by the developer.
* **California Privacy Rights:** California residents have the right to know what personal information is collected, the right to request deletion, the right to correct inaccurate data, and the right to non-discrimination for exercising privacy rights.
* **Do Not Track (CalOPPA):** Because Gbrowse does not collect tracking data or monitor user activity across third-party websites, the application does not alter its data handling practices in response to browser "Do Not Track" (DNT) or Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals.

#### C. Children's Privacy (COPPA & GDPR Article 8)
Gbrowse is a desktop software utility designed for general audiences and professionals. It is not directed to children under 13 years of age (or under 16 in the EEA/UK). We do not knowingly collect or solicit personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information to us, please contact us immediately.

### 7. DIAGNOSTICS & SYSTEM INFORMATION

Gbrowse includes a built-in "Export Diagnostics" utility in the **About** dialog. 
* This report is generated **strictly on demand** when clicked by the user.
* It contains technical environment metrics (application version, detected Chromium/Firefox binaries, OS architecture, profile counts, and system memory availability).
* **Privacy Redaction:** The diagnostic exporter strictly excludes profile names, tags, proxy hosts, passwords, cookies, and browsing history.
* Diagnostic data is copied to your local clipboard or saved to a local `.json` file. It is never automatically transmitted over the internet.
